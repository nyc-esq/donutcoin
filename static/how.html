How it works

Two pictures. The first is what a miner and a node do to make one block. The second is where your browser, your miner and the network sit relative to each other, and what each part is allowed to do.

1. Making a block

A miner asks a node for a template: the last block’s id, the current difficulty, the transactions waiting, and a coinbase paying 10,000 DONUT plus fees to the miner’s address. Each worker builds its own version of the block and hashes it with a different nonce, over and over, until the Scrypt hash falls under the target. That block goes to the node, which checks nine rules in order; if all pass, the node applies it, tells its peers, and every miner starts again on top of it.

2. Who talks to whom

Your browser talks to a public node through Cloudflare. It sends only signed transactions and signed market actions; the keys that sign them never leave your device. Your miner, if you run one, asks the same node for templates and hands back valid blocks; that is the whole permission system. Nodes trade blocks and transactions with each other and agree on the chain with the most work, within the checkpoint and cap rules. The operator’s own mining node is one of them and is not reachable from the internet.
