What am I looking at?

Princess Donut, the Queen Anne Chonk, named herself Queen and decided a queen ought to have a coin. This is the coin. Her steed Mongo carries the purse, and bears it with the quiet dignity of someone who would carry anything she asked him to.

Under the Crown it is a real proof-of-work blockchain: a block a minute, mined on ordinary computers by whoever turns up, counted in Ribbons, Tiaras and Crowns because those are the things a show cat actually wins. It is worth nothing on purpose: not money, not an investment, nothing to buy into. Coins are mined, never sold. This page is the ledger, updating every few seconds.

Said on the chain

Notes written into transactions, 280 characters at most. The block gives each one its time, the signature its author. To speak you need a licence (a block found in the last week), a Ribbon in hand, a @username and a 100 DONUT fee; one note per block. Post yours from the wallet. Every note has a permanent link on the Notes page, and there is an RSS feed.

Not money, not an investment, not for sale. Scrypt proof of work, one-minute blocks, 10,000 DONUT a block, never halves. Checkpoints pin history; a heavier chain can replace at most the last 20 blocks; the market settles at 21. Four units above the DONUT, base 10: Ribbon (1,000,000 DONUT, 100 blocks), Tiara (10 Ribbons, about a day of the network), Crown (10 Tiaras, about a week), Queen (10 Crowns, about ten weeks). The ladder stops at the Queen, because she does. Reward spendable after 3 blocks. Refreshes every 5 s.
